# The Shifting Basalts: Design Bible

Updated Nov 8, 2026 · @Brian Mahy

## Overview

The Shifting Basalts is a rogue-lite dungeon of columnar basalt that rebuilds itself every time someone exits, so no two runs share floors or rooms. Parties fight room to room, draft boons that grow into builds, and decide after each boss whether to extract or go deeper.

- **System:** D&D 5e, 2024 rules.
- **No XP:** the dungeon pays out loot and Quantum Basalts only, so it can't be farmed for levels. Parties level through striding and story, and the Basalts reshapes itself to match them, beckoning the challenge.
- **Entry levels 5 to 20:** higher entry levels earn greater rewards. A level 20 full clear pays differently than a level 5 full clear.
- **Stakes:** extracting keeps a share of the loot based on depth. Dying expels the character and locks them out for a time set by the dungeon's magic.

Items marked *(proposed)* came out of brainstorming and are not locked yet.

### Design principles

- **Evolutions should feel game-changing:** reaching one is a big moment, so it changes how a character plays rather than adding a few numbers.
- **Keep systems light:** avoid new tracks, currencies and bookkeeping math unless they clearly earn their place. Extra systems make play less engaging, not more.
- **No rests inside:** the party takes no short or long rests in the Basalts, which is why the spell slot recovery track exists.

* **Boons are echoes of striders:** a possessive boon name (Travert's, Elemain's, Ulma's) belongs to a past visitor whose technique Thils absorbed. Gods, places and monsters only appear in a boon's flavor or as a relic a strider carried, like the Token of Whim. Thils's Favor is the one exception: it's the god's own gift.

## Campaign context

The Shifting Basalts is one stop in The Archive In Between, a multiyear campaign. Unlike one-off destinations such as the All-color Prairie or Titan Canyon National Park, it's a place players can return to throughout the campaign.

- **The setting:** the players are striders for the Archive, which sits between all universes as A-0001, the prime universe. Stride gates make universe-hopping common enough for tourism, and the farther you stride, the weirder universes get.
- **Reputation:** the Archive fully knows the Basalts. It's a popular destination for glory seekers, get-rich-quickers and Archive researchers, and a holy place for locals, who welcome tourists because every visitor adds to the knowledge and skills of their god, Thils, the true god of mazes, traps and lost things.
- **The truth (DM only):** the Basalts is a fractured stride gate. It was built as a two-way gate to a paradise universe, then exploded and inverted. Runs should only hint at this; it pays off in the larger story.
- **Sonder:** the campaign's big bad, a multi-planar true god who exists only in the spaces you aren't looking at or trying to perceive. Glimpses in the Basalts point toward Sonder.
- **Gods:** the setting has true gods, false gods, demigods and single-plane gods, plus mixes of those.

* **Location (DM only):** the Basalts sits within the Labyrinth of Thilsavrass, per the Campaign Lorebook. A broken, lost stridegate is exactly what the labyrinth of the god of lost things collects.

- **Sonder glimpses (DM only):** never tracked in the app or shown on screen. Outside the Blind Architect fight, catching even a hint of Sonder takes a DC 35 Wisdom (Perception) check.

### Possible tie-ins *(proposed)*

- **The Shift:** the dungeon rebuilds when no one is perceiving it, which is where Sonder lives.
- **Glimpses:** come from negative space or from not looking, rather than from searching harder.
- **Named boons:** echoes of past visitors absorbed by the local god, which is also why boons can't leave the dungeon.
- **Lockout:** could double as Archive quarantine for striders who die inside.

## Run structure

A run is a stack of floors, each a set of rooms ending in a boss. After every boss the party chooses to extract with a share of the loot or descend to a harder floor.

- **Floors 1 to 4:** 4 rooms, then a boss room.
- **Post-boss room:** holds the party's purchased upgrades, such as the Forge and Healing Font, and is where they choose to descend or extract.
- **Floor 5:** a single legendary boss. Beating it is a full clear.

* **Rooms:** each cleared room triggers a boon draft (see Boon system rules). Floors 1 to 4 each hold 4 rooms and a boss room.
* **Bosses:** a boss closes every floor. Extraction is only possible after a boss.
* **The Shift:** every exit reshuffles floors, rooms and encounters, so a returning party never sees the same layout.
* **Death:** the character is expelled, is locked out, and forfeits this run's Quantum Basalts except passed ones. The party's loot pool stays whole as long as anyone extracts; only a wipe loses it (see Loot, extraction and death).
* **Encounter scaling** *(proposed)***:** encounters use the 2024 XP budget for the party's actual level plus each floor's offset, so entering low never makes a run easier, only less rewarding.
* **Difficulty curve** *(proposed)***:** each floor is keyed to an offset from the party's level, starting below it and ending well beyond it (see Difficulty by floor). Bosses use their floor's budget plus Shift Actions.
* **Shift actions** *(proposed)***:** lair-style terrain changes on initiative count 20, such as walls sliding, floors tilting or exits rearranging.

- **Escalation:** each floor raises enemy HP and damage, and room tricks (traps and hazards that damage players directly) grow more frequent and more complex.

### Difficulty by floor *(proposed)*

Floors are keyed to the party's level: the first sits below it, and the legendary boss sits well beyond it. A fresh party can't beat floor 5; it takes several runs of Devotions, upgrades and practice. Every run still accomplishes something, and there's always a reason to go back.

| Floor | Encounter level | Budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Party level −2 | Moderate |
| 2 | Party level −1 | High |
| 3 | Party level | High |
| 4 | Party level +2 | High |
| 5 (legendary) | Party level +4 | Double High |

- **Leveling up doesn't trivialize it:** the whole ladder moves with the party's level, so permanent power inside the Basalts comes from Devotions and upgrades, not character levels.
- **Playtest targets:** a fresh party should reach floor 2 or 3, most parties should reach floor 4 by their third to fifth run, and a first legendary clear should take around six to eight runs, usually after the 4th boon slot, the Forge and a few stat track ranks.
- **Tuning dial:** if parties hit the legendary boss too early or too late, adjust the floor 5 offset first, since it's the wall the whole progression builds toward.

### Run math *(proposed)*

A full run has 20 drafts (one per room on floors 1 to 4, boss rooms included) and 20 Devotions. A boon takes 3 picks to reach Level 3.

| By the end of | Drafts | With 3 slots | With 5 slots |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Floor 1 | 5 | 3 boons, two at Level 2 | 5 boons at Level 1 |
| Floor 2 | 10 | All 3 at Level 3, 1 spare pick | 5 boons at Level 2 |
| Floor 3 | 15 | 6 spare picks | All 5 at Level 3 |
| Floor 4 | 20 | 11 spare picks | 5 spare picks for combining and refilling |

- **Spare picks:** a 3-slot player maxes out on floor 2, so Pass converts spare picks into Quantum Basalts that extract even on death. Early players then earn the currency that buys more slots.
- **Reprieve:** replaces a room, so taking one costs a draft.

## Boon system rules

Each player starts with 3 boon slots and can buy up to 5 with Quantum Basalts. Every boon climbs Level 1 to 3, then either evolves in its slot or combines with a partner boon to free a slot.

### Drafting

- **Personal offer:** after each room, each player sees their own 4 boons, weighted toward their class and the tags they hold, and picks one. Two players can still end up with the same boon.
- **Hidden boon:** a 5th offer unlocks for every player when the room's secret condition was met, or for one player when they meet their class trigger. Hidden boons sit in their own extra slot and never level, evolve or combine; they're flavor for really playing your character. A player who earns a second hidden boon can replace the one they hold, or pass for the normal Pass bonus.
- **Offer mix** *(proposed)***:** 1 offer is a boon you already hold, so leveling stays within reach, 2 are random, and 1 is a wildcard (an Evolution or Combination if anyone qualifies).

### Draft choices

- **Replace:** drop a held boon entirely to take the new one.
- **Pass:** skip any draft, even with open slots, to earn 1 Quantum Basalt, times the entry-level multiplier. Passed Basalts are guaranteed to extract with the player, even if they die.

### Evolve or combine

- **Evolve:** a Level 3 boon becomes its Evolution and stays in its slot. This is the game-changing, high-ceiling path.
- **Combine:** two specific Level 3 boons fuse into one, opening a slot for a new boon. The new boon starts at Level 1, so the build gains variety but carries a weak boon into harder floors.
- **Combinations are final** *(proposed)***:** they can't evolve further, which keeps Evolution's ceiling higher.
- **Gating** *(proposed)***:** Evolutions and Combinations happen at a Forge room or through the wildcard offer.

### Stat tracks

- **Earned every room:** each room clear grants each player a Devotion to put toward a stat track they choose, separate from the boon draft. Devotions are named for the locals' reverence for the Basalts and their god. A few Devotions complete a rank *(proposed)*.
- **Permanent and dungeon-only:** tracks persist run after run, even after death, but only work inside the Shifting Basalts.
- **No slots:** tracks never use boon slots, never evolve and never combine.
- **Safe stats only:** tracks grow numbers 5e lets scale freely. Ability scores, skills, attack bonus, AC and save DCs are off limits to protect bounded accuracy.
- **Tier caps** *(proposed)***:** maximum ranks rise with the 2024 tiers of play, so higher-level runs have new ceilings to reach.
- **Payoff boons:** a tag in the regular boon pool. A payoff boon reads from a stat track, such as A Healthy Dose of Pain reading from Max HP, so drafting one shows players why their tracks matter. Payoff boons don't evolve or combine, because they already grow with their track. A payoff only appears in your offers once you have at least 1 rank in its track.

### Hidden boon conditions (examples)

- No one drops below half HP.
- An enemy is killed by terrain.
- The room is cleared in 3 rounds or fewer.
- Every player triggers a different boon tag.
- No spell slots are spent.

* **Class triggers** *(proposed)***:** each class can have a signature condition. Example: a fight ends with an enemy charmed, frightened or talked into surrender, and the Bard or Sorcerer earns Charm the Pants Off.

### Room condition pool (d20) *(proposed)*

The generator rolls one secret condition per non-boss room. Meeting it unlocks a hidden offer for every player, drawn from Thils's hidden gifts in the Boon catalogue. The Hidden Sight upgrade hints at it.

| d20 | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | No one drops below half HP. |
| 2 | An enemy is killed by terrain or a room trick. |
| 3 | The room is cleared in 3 rounds or fewer. |
| 4 | Every player triggers a different boon tag. |
| 5 | No spell slots are spent. |
| 6 | No one takes damage from a room trick. |
| 7 | Every player drops at least one enemy. |
| 8 | The last enemy falls to a critical hit. |
| 9 | No healing is used. |
| 10 | Every player uses a terrain feature, like a rune, column, cable or ledge. |
| 11 | An enemy is defeated by a room trick it armed itself. |
| 12 | Someone leaves an offering at a shrine to Thils mid-fight, and the shrine survives. |
| 13 | The true exit is found before the fight ends. |
| 14 | A player brings an ally back up from 0 HP. |
| 15 | No one misses an attack in the final round. |
| 16 | No player ends a turn more than 30 ft from an ally. |
| 17 | An Absorbed Echo is defeated by a player using the same boon it carried. |
| 18 | No one takes the Dash or Disengage action. |
| 19 | A player finishes the room Bloodied without being healed. |
| 20 | DM's choice: the party did something clever, in character or funny enough to earn it. |

### Supporting rules *(proposed)*

- **Tags:** boons carry tags (Core, Echo, Reaper, Spell, Crit, Stance, Volley, Shadow, Rally, Guard, Bond, Flurry, Blood, Hunt, Payoff) for offer weighting and Resonance.
- **Resonance:** when 2 or more players hold boons with the same tag, each gains a small shared rider. Keying it to tags keeps Resonance alive now that exact duplicate boons are rarer. See Resonance riders below.
- **Scaling:** boon values use proficiency bonus (PB) where possible, so one boon pool works from level 5 to 20. Offers only include boons a character can use, so no Spell boons for a character without spells and no Bond boons without a summon or companion.
- **Extra Attack:** per-hit riders trigger once per turn unless the boon says otherwise.
- **2024 hooks:** Weapon Mastery properties (Vex, Topple, Push) and the Bloodied state make clean boon triggers.

### Resonance riders

When 2 or more players hold at least one boon with the same tag, each of them gains that tag's rider. Core and Payoff tags are excluded, so Resonance stays a reward for build synergy.

| Tag | Rider |
| --- | --- |
| Echo | Once per round, when an Echo-tagged ally triggers an echo within 30 ft of you, your next hit this round deals +PB force damage. |
| Reaper | When any Reaper-tagged player drops an enemy, every Reaper-tagged player gains +10 ft speed until the end of their next turn. |
| Spell | Once per round, when a Spell-tagged ally casts a leveled spell, your next cantrip or spell this round adds +PB to one damage roll. |
| Crit | When a Crit-tagged ally scores a critical hit, your crit range expands by 1 until you score a critical hit or the room ends. |
| Stance | While two or more Stance-tagged players are within 15 ft of each other, both gain +1 AC. |
| Volley | Ranged attacks by Volley-tagged players ignore cover from other creatures and deal +PB damage against targets another Volley player hit this round. |
| Shadow | Enemies within 5 ft of a Shadow-tagged player take a −PB penalty to passive Perception, and Shadow-tagged allies don't reveal each other when attacking from the same cover. |
| Rally | When you grant temp HP, healing or a buff to another Rally-tagged player, you both gain PB temp HP. |
| Guard | When a Guard-tagged ally within 15 ft takes damage, you can use your reaction to take half of it for them, reducing the total by PB. |
| Bond | Summons and companions count as allies for other Bond players' features, and gain +PB to damage rolls within 10 ft of another Bond player's summon or companion. |
| Flurry | Hits other Flurry-tagged players land on your target this round count as 1 hit toward your own multi-hit thresholds, like The Express or Thousand Cuts. |
| Blood | While any Blood-tagged player is Bloodied, all Blood-tagged players gain +PB to weapon and unarmed damage rolls. |
| Hunt | When a Hunt-tagged player marks, studies or hits a boss or a Large or larger creature, the next Hunt-tagged player to attack it this round has advantage. |

### Balance and wording notes

- **Reaper's Sprint:** a guaranteed crit at Level 1 is strong for Rogues and Paladins, since Sneak Attack and Divine Smite dice double. Consider advantage plus crit on 18 to 20 at Level 1, with the auto crit at Level 2. Add a teleport range, such as 30 ft, and confirm whether "behind them" means the killed enemy or the next one.
- **Nothing Personal:** cap the chain at PB extra attacks per turn. Against low AC, a high-level fighter could otherwise chain a dozen times or more.
- **And You Get a Spell!:** free copies of Disintegrate or Power Word Kill will melt bosses. Consider a free copy once per room, then a copy costs a slot one level lower.
- **Critical Delight:** the Level 3 multiplier can reach ×5 with Extra Attack. Cap it at ×4 or reset it each turn. "Double" means the whole damage total is doubled.
- **Crits Are for Suckers:** the Level 2 step is small. Consider 25%, 35%, then 50%.
- **Travert's Echoes:** echoes trigger once per turn, even with Extra Attack. Still to decide: whether echoes hit allies, whether several echoes can hit the same enemy, how many echoes can be banked, and whether banked echoes expire after a room.
- **Chain of Echoes:** the final clause needs a pass to pin down which target gains the extra echo.
- **"Behind":** means physically behind the target. Some attacks only hit in one direction, so being behind also keeps you out of them.
- **Spell "hits":** a failed saving throw counts as a hit.

* **A Fine Steel's Glint:** define "less damage" (for example, damage reduced by PB at Level 1 and resistance at Level 2), the damage added per turn held (for example, +1 weapon die, up to PB turns), the Level 3 teleport range, and whether you can use reactions or cast spells in stance.
* **Stand Off:** the challenge is a contest using your highest ability against the attacker's same ability, so a Strength-based warrior rolls Strength against Strength. Counters don't use your reaction; instead, you can counter a number of times per round equal to the boon's level. Entering stand off is a free action, once per turn.
* **Overhealing:** 5e temp HP don't stack; new temp HP only replace old if higher. Decide the resistance type and duration, such as all damage until the start of the healer's next turn. It pairs with Spillover: Overhealing rewards big heals, and Spillover turns the excess into damage. Both stay separate boons.
* **Samurai's Wake:** set the lightning damage and decide whether the return hit is also guaranteed.
* **A Healthy Dose of Pain:** Level 1 now adds the HP your Max HP track grants, once per turn, so it grows with the track instead of adding full HP (around +90 a hit at level 10) to every attack.

* **Pale Lodge contracts** *(proposed)***:** the Lodge could list each run as a contract whose lethality rating rises with every Pressure, an in-world way to show the risk the party is choosing.

## Loot tables *(proposed)*

Loot is what someone lost. Thils collects lost things, so the Basalts pay out what past striders, pilgrims, tourists and the gate's builders left behind, not stone or fire gear. Floors 1 and 2 pay trinkets, consumables and mild gear, floor 4 pays rare items, and a full clear opens Thils's Vault, where each player drafts a build-defining relic.

## Quantum Basalts and permanent upgrades

Quantum Basalts are a rare resource awarded to each player on extraction. They are valuable in the wider universe, so every one spent on a permanent upgrade is one not sold or used elsewhere.

- **Payout:** every player receives the same amount, unless they've bought the Extra Loot upgrade. Basalts earned by passing a draft come on top of that and are guaranteed, even on death.
- **Tracking:** upgrades are bought and tracked per player, but their effects are shared with the whole party. Several owners in one party add uses (see Room types).
- **Cost curve** *(proposed)***:** escalating ranks priced against run payouts (see the upgrade table), so early unlocks feel quick and late ones feel earned.

### Pacing *(proposed)*

- **Typical run:** a tier 1 party extracting after floor 2 or 3 earns 13 to 25 Quantum Basalts each. A full clear earns 65, plus any passes.
- **First big buy:** the 4th boon slot (40) takes 2 to 3 typical tier 1 runs.
- **5th slot:** 120 takes about two tier 1 full clears, or one tier 2 full clear at ×2.
- **Passing:** worth 1 Basalt per pass at tier 1, against 5 to 25 per boss, so it's a safety net rather than a strategy.
- **Everything:** buying every rank of every upgrade costs about 1,000 Basalts, so players choose what to chase and still have reasons to sell Basalts elsewhere in the multiverse.

## Room types

Combat rooms drive the boon draft, and post-boss rooms are where Quantum Basalt upgrades show up mid-run.

| Room | What happens | When it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combat | A fight, then the shared boon draft and any hidden boon. | Most rooms on every floor |

| Boss | Closes the floor with shift actions, then extract or descend. | End of every floor |
| Forge | Level up a held boon, or evolve or combine eligible Level 3 boons. One use per owner in the party, then it cools. | Post-boss, once unlocked |
| Healing Font | Restores the party between floors. Each owner beyond the first adds temp HP for everyone (proposed: +5 each). | Post-boss, once unlocked |
| Reprieve | A safe room. Each player chooses Rest (a Short Rest's benefits, including Hit Point Dice and short-rest features, without the hour) or Reflect (level up one boon). | Mid-floor, by chance, once bought. ### Boss stat scaling *(proposed)*

Boss stats are built at the table from four inputs: party level, party size, floor and Pressures. The floor sets a target CR, the benchmark table turns that CR into numbers, and each boss's template adjusts them to fit its role.

| Floor | Target CR | Legendary Actions | Legendary Resistances |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Party level −1 | None | None |
| 2 | Party level +1 | None | 1 |
| 3 | Party level +2 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | Party level +4 | 2 | 2 |
| 5 (legendary) | Party level +7 | 3 | 3 |

- **Party size:** multiply boss HP by party size ÷ 4. For 6 or more players, add 1 Legendary Action.
- **Pressures:** Heavy Crown adds 1 Legendary Action on every floor, including floors 1 and 2.
- **DPR:** each template splits the benchmark damage per round across its attacks as percentages. Round each attack to the nearest dice expression.
- **Shift Actions:** use the boss's save DC.
- **Roster creatures:** when the generator needs a creature above what the roster offers, push it up CR with the same benchmarks.

### How the generator builds an encounter

- **Family weights by floor:** floor 1 is Wardstones and Mazework evenly. Floor 2 adds Echoes and the Lost. Floor 3 leans Gate-Born and Echoes, and floor 4 leans Inverted Paradise and Gate-Born.
- **Splash:** about 1 room in 4 adds one creature from a second family on that floor.
- **Filling the budget:** start with one brute or elite, add support roles, then fill the remainder with minions.
- **Elite rooms:** add one creature from the next floor's families, or push one creature 2 CR higher.
- **Locals never fight:** pilgrims and worshippers of Thils are never enemies.
- **High tiers:** levels 17 to 20 lean on the CR 10 to 16 elites in multiples. If that feels thin in play, this is the first place to add creatures.

### Size, elevation, exits and lighting

## App requirements

The app replaces the character sheet: characters are saved in it, and the DM loads whichever players are present at the start of a run.

### Screen layout

- **Main stage:** the current room fills most of the screen. It's generated from this Bible and the Campaign Lorebook: room type, layout and terrain, encounter, room tricks and hidden condition, plus the boss and its Shift Action on boss rooms.
- **Run tracker:** a smaller panel for floor and room progress, active Pressures, and the descend-or-extract choice at post-boss rooms.
- **Player sidebar:** one card per player that opens into their boon slots, levels, evolutions, combinations, hidden boon and suppressed boons; stat track ranks and Devotions; and their upgrade path, where they can buy upgrades with Quantum Basalts.
- **Group rewards:** run loot, Quantum Basalts earned and passed Basalts, tracked for the whole party.

### DM view and player view

The DM hosts the run; players join on their own devices with a lighter view.

- **DM view:** everything above, plus the generator controls. Roll floors and rooms, see full enemy and boss stats built from the benchmarks, roll loot and pull up any table, trigger room tricks and Shift Actions, see the room's hidden condition, and see every player's boons, tracks, upgrades and profile.
- **Player view:** the room as the party sees it, with no enemy stats, hidden condition or unsprung tricks; their own boons, drafts, stat tracks and upgrades, including buying upgrades; the loot assigned to them; their Quantum Basalts; and where the party is in the dungeon.

### Characters

- **Lean profile, not a full sheet:** characters level up through story between runs, so a saved full sheet would go stale. The app keeps only what it needs to run the dungeon, and each player confirms it at run setup: name, class and subclass, level (which sets PB), caster type (none, half, full or Pact Magic) with available slot levels, whether they have a summon or companion, and their main attack style (melee, ranged or spells).
- **Why those fields:** they set boon offer eligibility, class hidden boon triggers, every PB-based boon number, and spell slot recovery.
- **Modifiers, not totals:** the app shows what the dungeon adds, like +10 max HP or +20 ft speed, and players apply it to their own sheets. It never tries to track their full stats.
- **The dungeon layer:** boon slots, boon levels, evolutions and combinations; the hidden boon and any suppressed boons; stat track ranks and banked Devotions; upgrades and ranks; Quantum Basalts (banked, earned this run and passed); loot assigned to them; lockout timer; deepest floor reached; and run history.

### Runs

- Load the party, enter its level and size, and choose Pressures. Party level, party size and floor set encounter levels, boss stats, room trick DCs and the loot tier.
- Track floor, room, loot collected, and loot kept if the party extracted now.
- Track each player's boon slots (3 base, up to 5 with upgrades, plus a hidden boon slot), boon levels, and Evolutions or Combinations.
- Track boon counters such as banked echoes and Reaper chain jumps, plus each caster's slot recovery points and the slots they buy.
- Award each player a Devotion on every room clear, record deaths and start lockout timers automatically. Track each player's passed Basalts separately, since they survive death. Also track boons temporarily suppressed by bosses and room tricks, like the Inverted Reliquary and Memory Drain.

### Draft screen

- Show each player their own offer of 4, plus the hidden boon when its condition was met.
- Let each player pick, replace or pass, and level boons at the Forge.
- Flag players eligible to evolve or combine at a Forge.
- Apply rerolls and Banish from each player's upgrades.

### Data

- The boon catalogue, Combinations, Pressures and upgrades from this doc become the app's editable data, so new boons can be added without code changes *(proposed)*.

## Build clarifications

Rules the app follows where the rest of the Bible implies an answer without spelling it out.

1. **Leveling:** drafting a boon you already hold raises it 1 level, up to Level 3, and so does the Forge. The guaranteed held-boon offer picks from boons below Level 3; if all of a player's boons are maxed, that slot becomes the wildcard.
2. **Upgrade scope:** party-wide, with each extra owner adding more: Forge (+1 use per owner), Healing Font (+temp HP per extra owner), Extra Pressures (+5% rewards per owner) and Reprieve (every owner rolls). Party-wide at the highest rank only: Deep Pockets. Personal: Extra Boon Slot, Reroll, Banish, Starting Boon, Attunement, Hidden Sight, Quick Return and Extra Loot (its owner's Basalts only).
3. **Party level:** the average of the party's levels, rounded down. It sets boss CR, room trick DCs, the entry multiplier (one per party) and loot rarity. Encounter XP budgets add up from each player's own level, and PB-based boon numbers use each player's own PB.
4. **Payoff and Core boons:** they level through drafts and the Forge like other boons, but never evolve or combine.
5. **Tags:** an Evolution keeps its source boon's tag, and a Combination carries both source tags, for Resonance and offer weighting.
6. **Reprieve:** it replaces one of a floor's 4 rooms, so that floor has 3 combat rooms, the Reprieve and the boss. It grants no Devotion and no draft.
7. **The boon pool:** 69 boons across the main table and Batches 1 to 4. Kept separately: 49 Evolutions, 21 Combinations, 12 class hidden boons and 8 of Thils's hidden gifts.
8. **Death:** if even one player survives to extract, the party's loot pool stays whole and is shared as usual. Only a wipe loses it. A fallen character still forfeits the Quantum Basalts they earned that run, except passed ones.
9. **Pressure unlocks:** Brittle Ground, Narrow Seam and Closing Ring are available from the start. Extra Pressures rank 1 unlocks Restless Stone and Heavy Crown. Rank 2 unlocks the harsher versions, each replacing its base version *(proposed)*: Closing Ring II (the ring grows every 2 rounds, +25%), Brittle Ground II (the crack also knocks the player prone, +20%), Narrow Seam II (offers drop to 2, +25%), Restless Stone II (two Shift Actions per fight, +25%) and Heavy Crown II (+1 Legendary Action and +1 Legendary Resistance, +30%). Extra Pressures is party-wide, so one owner unlocks them for everyone, and every owner in the party (the first included) adds +5% to rewards.
10. **Pressure bonuses and passing:** Pressure bonuses multiply boss Quantum Basalt payouts (and loot), not Basalts earned by passing. A pass is always 1 Quantum Basalt times the entry multiplier.
11. **Stacking reward bonuses:** a player's boss Quantum Basalt bonus is the sum of the party's Pressures, +5% per Extra Pressures owner in the party, and their own Extra Loot (+10% per rank). The total is applied once and rounded up. Like Pressures, Extra Loot never applies to passed Basalts. Example: 40 Basalts with Pressures +25% and Extra Loot rank 2 (+20%) pays 40 × 1.45 = 58.
12. **Draft offers** *(proposed)*: first filter out ineligible boons. Spell boons, Quick Cantrip and Leyline Surge need spells; Bond boons need a summon or companion; Payoffs need at least 1 rank in their track; held boons (they have their own slot) and banished boons never appear. Then build the offer. Slot 1 is a held boon below Level 3: if you hold none, it becomes a weighted roll; if all of yours are maxed, it becomes a wildcard. Slots 2 and 3 are weighted rolls. Slot 4 is the wildcard: an Evolution or Combination you qualify for, or else one eligible boon at even odds. No more than 2 boons from the same tag appear in one draft. Narrow Seam drops a weighted slot first; Narrow Seam II drops the wildcard next. Weights start at 1 and multiply: ×3 for one of your class's primary tags, ×2 for a secondary tag, ×2 for a tag you already hold. Core boons stay at 1. The class tag table lives in `src/data/class-tags.json`; subclasses can shift it.
13. **Hidden offers** *(proposed)*: when a room's condition is met, each player gets a separate hidden offer of 2 random Thils's gifts they don't already hold, and picks one or passes for the normal Pass bonus. It's on top of the room's normal draft pick. Taking a hidden boon replaces any held one. A class trigger offers that class's hidden boon directly, with no roll; if both happen in one room, the player picks one of the three. The condition is revealed once it's met.
14. **Banish** *(proposed)*: each player keeps a standing list of up to 1, 2 or 3 boons (by Banish rank) that never appear in their offers, run after run. The list can only be edited between runs, never mid-run. It covers everything that rolls a boon, including the Starting Boon and the wildcard's random pick.
15. **Draft defaults** *(proposed, app choices)*: the wildcard is always the player's own Evolution or Combination, picked at random if several qualify. Elite rooms never drop the wildcard, even under Narrow Seam II. The next room can't be cleared until every living player has made their draft choice, and the DM can choose for any player.
